Reads arrive in bursts, writes leave in bursts, and the two sides rarely line up. The FIFO holds the difference, and tells the FSM when it can't take another full burst.

Inside dma_fifo

Data path: a 32-entry memory, written at wptr_q and read at rptr_q. The read is combinational (first word falls through), so the packer sees the oldest beat without a cycle of latency. Control: the two pointers and a count. Click a block to see its code.

Pointers and count

Each pointer wraps naturally (5 bits for 32 entries). The count says how many entries are held: +1 on a push alone, −1 on a pop alone, unchanged on both. clear (the engine's load) empties it at the start of a transfer, so beats left over from an aborted one can't leak into the next.

almost_fullmore than 16 helddma_ctrl won't issue a read: there's no room for a whole burst

The threshold is FIFO_DEPTH − MAX_BURST: below it a 16-beat burst always fits, so a read is never requested that would have to stall the bus.
