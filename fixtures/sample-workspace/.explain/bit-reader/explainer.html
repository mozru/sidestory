How bit_reader turns a stream of bytes, arriving last-first, into reads of 1 to 63 bits at a time.

The interface

Bytes arrive from a backward stream. The consumer asks for rd_bits bits at a time and always finds the next bits at the top of peek.

Two registers hold everything

buf_q is a 64-bit window whose valid bits are packed against the top. cnt_q counts them; 0 to 64 needs 7 bits, not 6.

Three one-line rules

All three outputs fall straight out of the count.

Take a byte only if it fitsin_ready = cnt_q ≤ W − 8
Serve a read only if enough bits are validavail = cnt_q ≥ rd_bits
The next bits are always at the toppeek = buf_q

Consume from the top

Reading rd_bits bits is a left shift: the bits read fall off the top and the count drops by the same amount.

Refill below the valid bits

A new in_byte is OR-ed in right under the last valid bit, shifted up by W − 8 − c. Consume runs first, so a read and a refill can share a cycle.
